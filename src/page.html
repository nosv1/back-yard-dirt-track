    Ways to connect these circles

    What I'm assuming

    • Measured on the satellite map: 164 × 282 ft, about 1.06 acres. The house, deck, and shed take a block about 88 × 40 ft inside the fence at the bottom left, and the track has to go around it.
    • The fire pit has an 8 ft keep-out and the electrical box a 4 ft one, both placed from the satellite photo. The hilly area is drawn but not used yet.
    • The solver always uses the roundabout, ridden in the direction you pick, or both. It tries every set of the other circles, in every order, ridden either way around. It throws out any loop where a straight crosses another straight or cuts through a circle, and any with less than 6 ft of grass between sections.
    • Noise: full throttle counts as 0 dB, part throttle as −7 dB, and braking as −14 dB, all measured at 50 ft. Sound then drops 6 dB each time the distance doubles. Past the "ease off" line, the sim rides at part throttle only (0.1 g of drive), the way you'd short-shift near the back. Neighbor doors are placed from the satellite photo: five back doors along and just past the back fence, the left (south) house about 75 ft out, and the right (north) house about 30 ft out. Each neighbor gets a care level: "most careful" counts the noise as heard, "aware" counts it as if they were twice as far away (−6 dB), and "don't mind" leaves them out. A layout scores by the loudest neighbor after that. The fence, wind, and which way the exhaust points aren't modeled.
    • Two circles ridden the same way are joined by the outside line, like a belt on two pulleys. Opposite ways are joined by the crossing line, which makes an S-bend.
    • Track edges stay 5 ft from the fence. The smallest radius allowed is 10 ft.
    • Lap time comes from a point-mass sim: a 40 mph cap, 0.3 g of drive, 0.45 g of braking, 0.7 g of grip in flat turns and 1.0 g on bermed ones. Treat it as a ballpark for comparing layouts, not a prediction.

    Questions for you

    • How much is quiet worth? "Length and quiet" trades 100 ft of track for each dB at the loudest neighbor you care about. Say if that feels off.
    • Who rides it, and how big are the jumps? That sets the smallest radius and how long a straight a jump needs.
    • What's already in the yard? Trees, the septic field, slope, and wet spots would become places no track can go.
